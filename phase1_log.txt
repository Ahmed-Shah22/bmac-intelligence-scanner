## Entry 7 — Phase 1 Milestone

**What I was trying to understand:** How to convert a DNA sequence into an N matrix,
and whether different promoter topologies produce different R-indices.

**What I did:**
1. Gathered 10 well-characterized E. coli promoters from iGEM and literature.
2. Classified them into three topology classes: constitutive (1 reaction),
   repressed (2 competing reactions), activated (2 sequential reactions).
3. Built the N matrix for each topology.
4. Computed the R-index for each using BindingAndCatalysis.jl.
5. Built the promoter + RBS combined model (2 independent reactions).

**What I learned:**
- The R-index is a topological invariant. All 7 constitutive promoters share the
  same R-index (~1/3) despite different sequences. The sequence determines K,
  not the topology, and the R-index does not depend on K.
- The repressed topology (lacUV5, pTet) has 8 positive regimes, sum ~1.000.
- The activated topology (pBAD) has 6 positive regimes, sum ~0.500. Half of
  parameter space collapses to zero. Sequential binding is more constrained
  than competitive binding.
- The promoter + RBS model (independent reactions) gives joint R-index = 1/9,
  confirming that independent reactions multiply.

**What I corrected:** Initially assumed the R-index would vary across promoter
sequences. It does not. It varies across topologies. This reframes Layer 1:
DNA -> circuit is a topology classifier, not a strength estimator.

**What is still open:** The catalysis layer (transcription and translation).
Full expression unit requires RNAP and ribosome to actually produce mRNA and
protein, not just bind.
